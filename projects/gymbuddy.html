GymBuddy

A simple start to fitness: one workout, a monthly target, a streak.

Product case study and V1 prototype, fitness. Full title: GymBuddy: making showing up feel achievable

The problem

  • Beginner gym-goers struggle to stay consistent because workouts feel overwhelming, time-consuming and hard to navigate. They join, don't know what to do, feel overwhelmed and stop going.
  • In a CDC survey of 525 men who did not meet or did not know whether they met physical-activity recommendations, 44.3% named lack of motivation as a barrier and 19.8% not having enough time. That is general-population context, not gym-specific.
  • Five beginner-fitness discussions on Reddit (r/beginnerfitness) reinforced it: heavy, confusing, time-consuming workouts and not knowing where to start.

What I did

  • I defined the V1 problem and target user: beginner or inconsistent gym-goers who feel overwhelmed, have limited time and struggle to stay motivated.
  • I sorted features into build now, build later and cut, keeping the pain point that recurred most, mattered most to consistency and could be tested fastest.
  • I built a V1 prototype with Claude covering onboarding, goal setting, workout completion and progress with a streak.

The decision

  • V1 is a simple workout, a monthly target the user sets and a streak.
  • Personalised AI is cut from V1. Without enough user behaviour data it would add complexity and make personalisation unreliable, so tracking behaviour and validating the core experience come first.
  • Also cut: social features, advanced nutrition tracking, complex analytics, a full personal-trainer replacement and a large content library.

What it shows about how I work

  • Cutting scope on purpose, and stating that the hypothesis is not yet proven.
  • Defining how it would be measured: the primary metric is core workout completion rate. Real-user validation is the next step.
