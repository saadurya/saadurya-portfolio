Zomato funnel and cohorts

Where first-time orderers leak, and which leak to fix first.

Funnel, cohort and metrics analysis, food delivery. Full title: Where Zomato's food delivery journey loses people

The problem

  • A first-time orderer in a Tier-1 city (Bengaluru, Mumbai, Delhi-NCR, Hyderabad, Chennai or Pune) passes through seven stages between opening the app and receiving the order. I wanted to know where users leak and which leak to fix first.
  • Zomato does not publish funnel or cohort data, so every stage rate, retention figure and segment share in my analysis is a planning figure, to be replaced by Phase 1 baseline data.
  • On those figures, 29 of every 100 app opens end in a delivered order, and Menu → Cart loses 21 users, 30% of those who viewed a menu.

What I did

  • I wrote one hypothesis for each stage (H1 to H7) and built a metric ladder for each: input, output, leading, lagging and guardrail.
  • I built the cohorts so retention is not baked into the definition. Users are grouped by Month-0 behaviour first (early-frequent, offer-led, everyone else), and retention is read afterwards.
  • I ranked segments by users lost per 100 new users, not by the lowest retention rate alone. “Everyone else” is half of all new users and loses more of them than either of the other two groups.

The decision

  • Fix Menu → Cart first (H3): popular-dish tags, portion size and the all-in price on menus. The target is 70% to 73%, which lifts delivered orders per 100 app opens from 29 to about 30.3.
  • Right behind it is new-user activation (H8), because “everyone else” loses the most users after the first month.
  • Delivery is not left last. If new users with a late first order retain 10 points worse at month 1, delivery for first orders becomes Priority 1.
  • Every test reports contribution margin per order, so a conversion win bought with discounts does not pass.

What it shows about how I work

  • Ranking by what a leak costs and how fast it can be tested, not by which number looks worst.
  • Cohort definitions that keep the outcome out of the definition, and guardrails with stop thresholds that cover what the business could lose.
  • Marking every figure as sourced, calculated or a planning figure.
