Growth strategy, travel marketplace

A product-led growth engine for Airbnb India

  • Growth
  • Strategy
The problem
Four growth loops looked equally attractive, but only one of them compounds without paying for incentives.
What I decided
Test one bet first: Guest → Host demand intelligence, run as a manual report with no engineering. Creator referral, travel-intent discovery and hidden-gem demand signals wait until that evidence exists.
What it shows
Prioritising by cost to test, and marking which claims are hypotheses and which are sourced.

Product case study, quick commerce. Group work with Syed Falzuddin

Raising order value at Zepto's Cart Review

  • Growth
The problem
Zepto leads on orders per store (2,140 a day against Blinkit's 1,425) but trails on order value. The cart decides what counts as “enough” by location and demand, not by what the shopper is buying.
What I decided
Build a personal milestone ladder first, replacing the fixed ₹499, ₹699 and ₹899 rungs. Two more solutions follow. One shopper type gets none of them: the Emergency Buyer, because pushing a one-item mission to add more puts the low-ticket orders that drive store throughput at risk.
What it shows
Choosing what to build first, and saying plainly who a solution does not serve.

Product strategy, B2B AI platform

From AI that answers to AI that finishes the job

  • Strategy
  • AI
The problem
Support AI can reply to a customer, but a post-purchase issue is only resolved when the order system confirms it. Each human-handled contact costs about $8.
What I decided
Start with read-only order and delivery resolution for mid-market e-commerce brands, on top of the helpdesk they already run. At a 25% automation rate, every 10,000 tickets a month saves $20,025 against $3,750 of platform cost, 5.3 times over.
What it shows
Picking a wedge and a segment, pricing it with stated inputs against Zendesk, and naming the evidence gap: my interviews were with four small founders, not mid-market buyers.

PRD, short-video platform in India

PulseReel Circuit Breaker

  • Specs
The problem
One unverified reel became tied to a nationwide protest movement within 72 hours, and the recommender had no way to tell ordinary virality from fast mobilisation.
What I decided
Detect spread patterns, never political viewpoints. Use in-app viewer feedback as one signal, not as proof, and keep every consequential decision reviewable and reversible.
What it shows
Writing a spec with firm boundaries, including what the system is not: an investigation or fact-checking tool.

Product case study and V1 prototype, fitness

GymBuddy: making showing up feel achievable

  • Built
  • Research
The problem
Beginners join a gym, don't know what to do, feel overwhelmed and stop going. In a CDC survey of 525 men who weren't meeting activity guidelines, 44.3% named lack of motivation as a barrier.
What I decided
V1 is a simple workout, a monthly target the user sets and a streak. Personalised AI is cut until there is behaviour data to justify it. I built a V1 prototype with Claude, and testing with real beginners is the next step.
What it shows
Cutting scope on purpose, and stating that the hypothesis is not yet proven.

System design brief, food delivery

Taking QuickBite from one city to 2 million users

  • Systems
  • Specs
The problem
Growth of 400 times in 12 months with an engineering team of fewer than 15. At peak there are about 670 browse reads a second against about 4 order writes a second.
What I decided
Decide what can never be lost (orders, payments, refunds) and what can drift (ratings, ETAs). Run 99.9% availability overall and 99.99% only for order placement and payment, where a failure loses transactions.
What it shows
Working through trade-offs with engineering instead of listing requirements.

PRD, fitness app

Connect & Commit: rewards and challenges for VitaFit

  • Specs
  • Growth
The problem
Users lose motivation after the first excitement, and on-demand workouts feel transactional, with no lasting link to an instructor.
What I decided
Two features: points and rewards for consistent actions, and instructor-led community challenges, including a prompt for instructors to send a short personal message at milestones. North Star: share of active users completing 3 or more workouts a week.
What it shows
Tying metrics to behaviour, and planning to confirm internal numbers before building.

Market research, ride-hailing

The pickup gap: what goes wrong between booking and boarding

  • Research
The problem
In a LocalCircles survey of about 3,500 frequent users in Bengaluru (Aug 2024), 85% of Uber customers had met at least one issue in 12 months and 46% reported a driver cancelling. Airports, malls, housing complexes and peak-hour streets are where riders and drivers fail to find each other.
What I decided
Research before solutions: public accounts, surveys, regulations and my own earlier driver interviews, then three research-led ideas for a more reliable pickup. Where only Uber holds the numbers, I left the sizing open.
What it shows
Scoping tightly (music, AC, safety and pricing are out) and labelling what I could not know.

Product teardown, beauty e-commerce

Why shoppers finish the decision outside Nykaa

  • Research
The problem
I picked one lip balm from 575+ options and spoke to three other shoppers. Three of the four of us checked options outside the app before deciding.
What I decided
Three bets to keep the decision inside the app. The evidence behind them: a Dermatologically Tested filter that listed 4 products and missed one I knew qualified, and a shade preview that never showed my shade worn.
What it shows
Reading a small sample honestly (four people, directional only) and following up when an answer was unclear.

UX evaluation, beauty shopping app

Heuristic evaluation and redesign of Purplle's shopping flow

  • UX
The problem
Eight usability issues between search and payment, including a Deals arrow that does nothing, an extra Category Stores layer, colour swatches that don't match their names and out-of-stock items mixed into listings.
What I decided
Ranked all eight against heuristics and UX laws, then mocked up the three highest-impact fixes: an In stock only filter, a decluttered cart and simpler category navigation.
What it shows
Tying every complaint to a named principle and a concrete recommendation.

Built with Claude Code, tutoring website

BrightSpring: a tutoring site with tooling that checks itself

  • AI
  • Built
The problem
A site built with AI drifts as it grows unless the rules are written down and checked.
What I decided
A design system, validated content, a design-reviewer subagent and a custom skill. I proved each tool by breaking things on purpose: the reviewer caught 3 of 3 planted violations and the validator rejected all 4 broken data files.
What it shows
Directing AI to build reusable tooling, then testing that the tooling really works.

AI agents, SaaS customer support

Skill, agent, or neither?

  • AI
  • Built
The problem
Five support capabilities, and the wrong choice of tool means an expensive mistake goes uncaught.
What I decided
Format goes in skills, volume in agents, risk in code. The result was 2 skills, 2 agents and 1 “neither”. A script rejected a draft that repeated a customer's “40%” before it could reach a reply.
What it shows
Deciding where AI belongs by what a miss costs and what would catch it.

Funnel, cohort and metrics analysis, food delivery

Where Zomato's food delivery journey loses people

  • Growth
  • Research
The problem
Seven stages from opening the app to receiving the order in Tier-1 cities, with a hypothesis for each drop-off.
What I decided
Priority areas: menu to cart first, then cart to checkout, then delivery experience. The numbers are labelled as assumptions until Zomato's own data can confirm them.
What it shows
Funnel and cohort analysis, a North Star with ladder metrics, and flagging assumptions as assumptions.
