Zepto Cart Review

Why baskets stop at “enough”, and who each fix is for.

Product case study, quick commerce. Group work with Syed Falzuddin. Full title: Raising order value at Zepto's Cart Review

  • Growth
  • +29% to +40%order growth a typical order needs before the cart pays a first ₹10 reward at ₹499
  • ₹357 to ₹388typical order value, from public estimates
  • 2,140 vs 1,425orders per store per day, Zepto against Blinkit
  • 84%of 59,049 sensitivity runs rank the personal ladder first

The problem

  • Zepto leads on orders per store, 2,140 a day against Blinkit's 1,425 in Q4 FY26, but trails on order value, at about ₹357 to ₹388 a typical order.
  • The cart sets “enough” by location and demand, not by what the shopper is buying, and its add-ons are unrelated to the basket.
  • On our cart walk-through the first reward rung at ₹499 needed 29% to 40% growth over a typical order. The add-ons below the fold were unrelated, and one slot was labelled Ad. That is one member's cart in one session, not a sample.

What I did

  • I did this as group work with Syed Falzuddin. We captured the cart on one member's account, read analyst and news reporting, and used public user comments, labelled as such.
  • We ran five whys down to a root cause, then ranked five blockers by how close they sit to Cart Review and how direct the evidence is. Two act at Cart Review, one is a constraint and two are set aside.
  • We described four behaviour-based segments (Emergency Buyer, Threshold Optimizer, Habitual Reorder, Planned Shopper) and held them as hypotheses until Zepto's own cohort data confirms them.

The decision

  • Build Solution 1 first: a personal milestone ladder in place of the fixed ₹499, ₹699 and ₹899 rungs. Quick Lists and cart add-on relevance follow.
  • RICE scores with stated inputs: ladder 17.3, Quick Lists 3.3, add-on relevance 3.1. The ladder ranks first in 84% of 59,049 re-scored combinations. Solutions 2 and 3 are a tie, so I sequenced them by dependency.
  • The Emergency Buyer gets neither the ladder nor Quick Lists, only one capped add-on row. Pushing a one-item mission to add more risks the low-ticket orders that drive store throughput.

What it shows about how I work

  • Choosing what to build first, and saying plainly who a solution does not serve.
  • Stating inputs up front. The segment shares of 35 / 25 / 25 / 15 are placeholders, the impact and effort figures are our estimates, and order-value figures are defined differently across sources.
  • Naming the guardrails before launch: cart-to-pay conversion, orders per store per day and contribution per order.
