Orbitly: skill or agent?

Five support capabilities, each placed where its failure can be caught.

AI agents, SaaS customer support. Full title: Skill, agent, or neither?

  • AI
  • Built
  • 2 / 2 / 1skills, agents and “neither”
  • 375words in the at-risk briefing built from three systems, with no data from the look-alike customer
  • 30 → 45day policy window changed with the skill untouched, and the reply flipped from refusal to refund
  • 8tickets graded and returned as one scorecard

The problem

  • Orbitly is a SaaS customer-support project with five capabilities: triage, replies, an at-risk briefing, weekly QA and bug reports. The wrong choice of tool means an expensive mistake goes uncaught.
  • Instructions in a skill or in CLAUDE.md are requests, not guarantees. So the question is not whether an LLM can do the job. It is what a miss costs and what would catch it.

What I did

  • I asked three questions of every capability, in order. Is the step structured and deterministic? Is it a known, reusable format and lightweight? Is it goal-driven and heavy on tools or sources?
  • I built each component and tested it standalone through the Claude Code CLI, in a fresh copy of the project that excludes the tests folder so the answer key was never visible. There is no orchestration anywhere.
  • Two skills (reply-drafter and bug-report-writer) and two agents (at-risk-briefing and ticket-quality-review, both with read-only tools). Triage is documented, not built.

The decision

  • Format goes in skills, volume in agents, risk in code. The result was 2 skills, 2 agents and 1 “neither”.
  • Triage is “neither”. A missed legal or security keyword is costly, so deterministic recall beats probabilistic judgement.
  • Weekly QA is an agent, not a skill. Grading is skill-like, but the job is volume, so the bulk ticket text stays out of the main session.
  • A script, check_figures.py, rejects any money, percent or duration figure that is not in the policy. It rejected a draft that repeated a customer's “40%” before it could reach a reply.

What it shows about how I work

  • Deciding where AI belongs by what a miss costs and what would catch it.
  • Testing each piece on its own, and saying which checks are deterministic and re-runnable. The model outputs vary slightly between runs.
